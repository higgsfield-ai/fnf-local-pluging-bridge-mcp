# Observed Failure Patterns

## A safe correction can still be incomplete

When residue survives a foreground-safe correction, compare the source feature with the full-strength support and transition. Repeating a decrement cannot remove a feature beyond its support. Extend only source-confirmed coverage and review the new mixed boundary; do not turn a selected transition width into a universal preset.

When a source-trimap intervention reduces leakage, inspect the complete residual and neighboring material before extending it. A successful central removal can leave a peripheral crescent, and the network can change output beyond the guide. Attribute the intervention to the input rather than to unchanged model weights. A bounded improvement does not establish a generally sufficient guide shape.

## Dense material and optical mixtures need different treatment

When a plausible silhouette loses a small attached or disconnected feature, inspect its source material and requested inclusion. Dense illuminated skin, reflective accessories, and thin fabric need material-specific treatment. Separate them from reflections on excluded objects rather than restoring every small component or removing every bright pixel.

When completing a hair bend produces a rigid hook, inspect the optical density and width assigned to that material role. Correct geometry, correspondence, and alpha texture require separate evidence. For a missing internal connector, check whether an exterior falloff was applied to hair-to-garment contact. Preserve material roles rather than treating every contour as background exposure.

## More optimization can make the result worse

When increasing a known-region loss reduces background error, also inspect opaque undercoverage in dense details. Reject a material regression even if aggregate numbers improve. Inspect the relevant tradeoff before accepting more training.

When a small subject part reappears, check its source visibility and original trimap before attributing an omission to forced background. An uncertain core can still be missed by the model. An editorial repair of that feature does not fix other gaps or prove improved generalization. Keep final delivery fixes separate from raw evaluation, especially on held-out footage.

## Temporal seams often come from assembly

When source-corresponding material jumps at an authoring handoff, inspect the regional application weight and continued visibility before tuning the solver. Correct support and role continuity where a processing interval ends before the source feature does. Treat a smaller temporal residual cautiously when missing material is being propagated into neighboring frames.

When alpha freckles follow spatial donor-confidence variation, isolate confidence from alpha samples and correspondence in a bounded comparison. A confidence-only correction is appropriate only when that weight variation causes the artifact. It is not permission to blur every difficult matte.

## Color and alpha must remain coherent

When an almost zero additive alpha increase produces a visible color jump, check whether the producer incorrectly uses a signed replacement color rule. Drive additive premultiplied color with the delivered quantized increment. A signed temporal operation can legitimately change foreground contribution without the same additive gate. Preserve each producer's algebraic meaning.

When AE decontamination preserves alpha, still inspect channel changes in opaque pixels. Unchanged alpha does not establish unchanged skin color. Separate lower-hair treatment from crown, arm, and shirt contamination, and compare source and composites for each region.

## Reuse evidence without manufacturing certainty

When output pixels are exactly unchanged, carry forward matching earlier regional review with its spatial and temporal limits. A passed native-transfer check cannot close later visible jitter feedback. Keep the old result's history, update current acceptance, and improve only the lesson supported by the new observation.
