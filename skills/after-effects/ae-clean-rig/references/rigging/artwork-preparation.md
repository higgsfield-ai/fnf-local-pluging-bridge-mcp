# Artwork preparation

## Registration and separation

Read this reference for a new flattened illustration, changed topology or repaired overlaps. Establish one source-to-working-coordinate conversion from the actual image dimensions. Preserve alignment, layer bounds and overscan when importing or precomposing; display-size coordinates are not source pixels.

Identify parts from their independent movement and occlusion. A portrait normally needs separate body or clothing, neck, head base, rear hair, front hair and moving locks, plus independent facial assemblies. A costume character may need different sockets and shell layers instead of a conventional neck. Separate background and decorative elements before deforming the character. Each part needs a complete alpha, useful pivot and clear ownership.

Choose reference-preserving raster cutouts for distinctive texture and linework. Native vector reconstruction suits simple shapes when it can retain the character's silhouette, palette and expression; inspect the reconstructed neutral pose against the source before accepting simplification. Do not silently redraw a detailed illustration into a different style.

## Hidden surfaces

Remove the old pixels of an extracted moving feature from its underlying surface. Keeping an intact original beneath moving hair, eyes or mouth creates a stationary duplicate even if the neutral pose looks correct. Retain an untouched reference for comparison, outside the rendered character.

Reconstruct skin beneath hair, neck beneath the jaw and collar, clothing beneath limbs, and complete roots beneath overlapping parts. Continue color, texture and lighting across the hidden region without drawing an ink seam across a concealed joint. Size overlap for the planned range of motion, not a fixed pixel allowance copied from another character.

When reconstruction needs image editing, discover an operation accepting the source image, edit region and preservation instructions. Use it for the missing surface, retain accepted visible artwork, inspect the result and register it to the source before compositing. Respect the user's chosen provider and its actual capability contract. A newly generated full portrait is not a substitute for a missing patch unless redesign was requested.

## Preparation gate

Render the neutral assembly and compare proportions, silhouette, facial placement, edges and color to the reference. Temporarily move adjacent parts through intended extremes before making Puppet meshes. Inspect newly revealed pixels, roots and precomposition bounds for holes, doubled features, visible patch edges or clipped tips. An exploded-parts sheet demonstrates separation but does not prove sufficient hidden coverage. Repair uncovered regions, restore neutral and save a preparation checkpoint.
