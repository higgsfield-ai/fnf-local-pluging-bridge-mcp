# Scope Diagnostics

## Measurement context

Establish the actual source and processing stage, output, Scopes LUT, Viewer and monitoring LUTs, scale, and display settings. Selecting a node does not prove that the scope measures its input. Highlight behavior in the Viewer does not automatically establish the same scope tap. Organize reversible stage comparisons when a processing fault needs localization.

Video Level Scopes and the displayed unit scale change scope presentation rather than pixel encoding, media bit depth, or the physical monitor. An HDR scale requires the corresponding signal interpretation; its nits are not a measurement of the actual display or scene illumination. Do not transfer SDR targets to Log, PQ, or HLG automatically.

## What each scope establishes

Waveform preserves horizontal position while combining pixels from different image heights in each column. Y-prime is coded luma, not a camera exposure meter. A white overlap of RGB traces may combine different pixels and does not prove a neutral object. Parade compares channels but does not require their overall peaks to coincide in a colored scene.

Vectorscope shows color-difference direction and magnitude, not a percentage of perceived saturation. Its center combines black, gray, and white; use waveform to distinguish their levels. Targets represent reference signals, not the full gamut boundary or a required extent for a normal image. Skin Tone Indicator is a hue aid, not a brightness, saturation, or identity standard.

Histogram shows value distribution without spatial location. Similar histograms can conceal a different subject exposure. CIE chromaticity shows a two-dimensional relation to a selected gamut; points inside its triangle do not certify the entire color volume at every luminance.

Display Qualifier Focus marks sampled pixels when the Viewer qualifier cursor is positioned appropriately. It does not require creating a correction mask and does not change the measurement point to the selected node input.

## Comparable display settings

Record the actual channels, quality, units, zoom, tonal ranges, Low Pass, Extents, and trace settings before comparing. Low Pass can suppress visible extremes while Extents reveals them. Trace and graticule brightness do not change image levels. Vectorscope two-times zoom changes apparent radius without increasing image saturation.

Separate global scope settings from each panel's selected type and controls. Mid-range vectorscope isolation is not a skin mask. Reference levels do not limit the signal. A graph can extend beyond the visible zoomed area, so inspect the range before declaring clipping or containment.

Use an ordinary current frame without a comparison overlay for baseline A/B. Verify that the trace updates after seeking or switching versions. A stale or mismatched scope is a limitation of the observed comparison, not proof of a general application defect.
