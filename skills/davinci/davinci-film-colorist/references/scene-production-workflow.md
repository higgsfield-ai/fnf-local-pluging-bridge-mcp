# Scene Production

## Establish the visual task

Inspect the requested available fragment and identify scenes and lighting conditions. Establish the main action or subject, relevant product properties, intentional lighting, supporting surroundings, and competing bright or saturated regions. Separate an observed source from a hypothesis about its cause. Describe desired tonal relationships, material color, and acceptable texture, together with the visible conditions that would make the grade unsuccessful.

Use references with comparable light, materials, scale, and narrative purpose. When deeper research is requested and material is available, compare several scenes to distinguish a recurring relationship from an exception. A limited correction does not require a new reference search. Display JPEG values cannot establish the user's camera signal or portable exposure targets.

## Prepare and prototype

Use the established input, pipeline, scope, and return state from the entrypoint and selected technical references. Balance comparable shots while retaining intentional differences in light. Evaluate needed early denoising through the actual output and reassess its final strength once the look reveals or conceals defects. Separate exposure placement from contrast strength.

Choose available representative frames that challenge the look through skin or product color, framing, lighting, and saturated materials. For a single clip, use relevant moments as its light or movement changes. Begin variants from the same prepared baseline and retain the identity, timecode, and purpose of each comparison. A Gallery still is not a local grade version of every clip.

Evaluate the proposed look across the selected frames. Do not choose it solely because it is brighter. A matched-brightness comparison can diagnose color differences, but must not erase an exposure difference that the task is intentionally testing.

## Refine and match

Place targeted light and color work in the designated secondary stages. Choose selections for a visible need and inspect intersections, softness, tracking, and their effect through the global look. If the look repeatedly damages different scenes, revise the common look instead of accumulating compensating masks on each shot.

Returning to white balance or exposure after evaluating the look is expected: decision order is iterative while the agreed signal graph remains intact. Match corresponding materials and motivated light across cuts. Different framing or window area can change the overall scope trace without indicating a mismatch. Preserve justified day, night, source-color, and narrative changes.

## Texture and acceptance

Add selected grain, halation, bloom, or softness only after the color structure is accepted. Establish each effect's input domain and separate its color and texture contribution from an existing look. The supplied LUT look does not provide these spatial or temporal effects by itself.

Inspect ordinary viewing size and useful detail near one-to-one pixel scale. Check available motion, occlusions, frame edges, and cuts for lost texture, unwanted clipping, color jumps, halos, trails, drifting keys, and inconsistent grain. Compare the same frames and output conditions. Verify saved state, active version, control-frame readback, and restoration of ordinary diagnostic modes.

Record the chosen visual intent, changed operation, observed benefit or side effect, tested material and motion, user feedback, and remaining limitation beside the task. Lack of feedback is not artistic approval. Keep control access, visible benefit, temporal quality, and successful saving as separate claims.
