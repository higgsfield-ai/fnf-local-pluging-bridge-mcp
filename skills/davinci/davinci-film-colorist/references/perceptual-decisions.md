# Choosing Correction Strength

## Contrast and depth

Separate overall tonal character, spatial relationships among objects, and transition shape near shadows and highlights. Inspect the prepared image through the accepted look and output before adding contrast. A compulsory S-curve can duplicate contrast already present. Global contrast does not substitute for spatially specific light relationships.

Compare relevant subject and background regions while preserving the intended lighting. Dense shadows can remain differentiated; lifted shadows can lose shape. A softened highlight should retain useful texture and source identity. Evaluate an expressive candidate and its weaker form rather than defaulting to either maximum restraint or maximum separation.

## Saturation and hue

Distinguish an unwanted hue from excessive saturation. Global desaturation can remove useful separation while leaving the wrong hue relationship intact. Inspect skin, clothing, neutrals, and saturated highlights at similar perceived brightness when diagnosing color. Do not erase an intentional exposure difference merely to make variants equally bright.

Hue, saturation, and luminance affect perceived contrast and material identity together. More chroma is not automatically a richer image. Preserve differences among neighboring colors and examine whether a selected correction also reaches a different important object. Texture and light can explain apparent color variation that should remain.

## Split tone and film character

Define which tonal ranges and neutral areas the split should influence. Protect Neutrals refers to low chroma rather than skin or middle brightness. An untouched Log Midtone does not guarantee an untouched face, whose shadows and highlights span different values.

Inspect a neutral material, colored midtone, skin, and a source highlight. Reduce strength or adjust the tonal distribution when the result damages important colors. Global color selection cannot perfectly distinguish objects with matching hue, saturation, and tone. Reconsider the global look when subject protection and the requested style conflict rather than promising exact separation.

Treat film character as a combination of tonal shape, palette, saturation behavior, highlight transitions, and optional texture. A LUT name does not prove a film model. Establish which components are already present and avoid doubling a curve, print rendering, grain, or halation. Evaluate the scene rather than assigning one recipe to every lighting condition.

## Noise and temporal evidence

Select noise processing by the balance between distracting variation and useful detail. Inspect the native image at useful scale and through the final look. Distinguish a control's accepted value, a visible static effect, and temporal quality. A still, a scope change, or a numerical improvement alone cannot certify a moving grade.
