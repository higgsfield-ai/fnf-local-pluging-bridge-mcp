# Comparing Creative References

Choose supplied references by lighting, material, composition, and intended effect. If a project or creator is named, investigate it as current task input. Do not inherit fixed titles, creators, or brands from historical research as default creative targets.

Compare several relevant views when available to identify stable relationships and meaningful exceptions. Observe whether skin keeps motivated colored light, whether form depends on reflected edges rather than brightness, and whether pale environments intentionally relax the black point. A common look can adapt to daylight, interior mixed light, and darkness without assigning identical skin levels or scope traces.

Separate material color from light color and environmental filtering. A vivid fabric, a colored source, and an underwater cast need not be produced or corrected by the same operation. Similar hues do not establish the same LUT, and different images do not prove different LUTs.

Convert each useful observation into a question for the current material and a condition that could disprove the proposed approach. Check whether preserving highlight texture supports the story, whether extra face brightness is necessary, or whether a global saturation change erases useful color relationships. These are decision conditions, not copied production recipes.

Distinguish a creator's documented explanation from the observer's visual interpretation. Credits do not establish who made every individual correction. A still or sample frame does not reveal the node graph, source profile, temporal treatment, or entire scene. Record the actual access obtained and use the user's available material when a reference service is unavailable; do not require a subscription solely because historical research used one.
