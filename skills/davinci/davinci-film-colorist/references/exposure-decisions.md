# Exposure Decisions

Separate recorded exposure and recoverable information from the brightness of a chosen display rendering. Establish what the viewer should distinguish, what can remain hidden, and which motivated light connects the shots. A waveform level or average brightness does not choose the final grade.

Compare meaningful regions and the tonal distance between them. A narrow range of dark tones and a narrow range of light tones can both be restrained. Choose stable or changing tonal relationships according to the scene rather than assuming that greater contrast is always more dramatic.

For an interior with a bright window, decide whether outside texture serves the scene or competes with the interior. Similar peak levels can conceal very different distributions of attention. For underexposed material, weigh additional readability against revealed noise and damaged texture. Stop raising the image when the information gained no longer helps or requires destructive smoothing.

Distinguish essential material texture from a small specular reflection or a direct source. Preserve useful transitions without flattening every bright object into gray. Making a clipped area darker or adding a glow does not recover its lost gradation.

Shape local exposure around plausible light and form rather than precise object outlines. Inspect internal blacks so an illuminated object does not acquire a milky base. When darkening a wall or surrounding area, preserve the relationship between a practical source and the surface it illuminates. A mask that looks natural on a still must also survive motion, occlusion, and changing shape.

Do not reveal all shadows merely to maximize visible information. Shadows, silhouettes, and atmospheric contrast can separate depth and control attention. Similar stated source temperatures do not guarantee matching spectral rendering across mixed light; inspect each region before applying a global correction.

Preserve material identity through reflections, highlight shape, and microshadows. Treat matte cloth, glossy paint, glass, and metal according to their visible surface behavior. Removing every reflection can erase that identity.

Compare reversible choices at the same frame, scale, and output. Bypass only the correction whose contribution is being judged. A camera-log versus final-grade pair does not isolate one exposure or mask operation. Stop when the agreed visual relationship is achieved without unacceptable damage to light, material, or motion.
