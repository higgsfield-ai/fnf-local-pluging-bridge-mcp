# Native Look Techniques

Use these techniques only for a requested native alternative or a specific adjustment within the established process. They describe choices, not tested universal recipes. Keep subject selections and local light work in secondary before the global look, preserve the agreed signal domains, and judge every change through the actual output.

## Color separation and density

For restrained skin with saturated surroundings, establish whether hue and saturation separate the materials. A Warper selection can use that distinction while protecting nearby colors. If they overlap in hue and chroma, consider verified luminance or spatial separation. Every later targeted correction needs its own valid selection or an explicit incoming key; serial color processing does not transmit a subject mask automatically.

For dense color, compare its luminance and saturation rather than simply lowering all channels or maximizing chroma. Preserve differences among saturated materials and inspect highlights. Subtractive saturation, ColorSlice Density, and a tonal curve have different consequences. A desired change in hue must be reevaluated for perceived lightness and material identity.

For monochrome, different colors can map to similar gray. Adjust the relevant channel contribution or preconversion relationships to preserve form, checking noise and transitions. A monochrome image does not remove the need to verify source encoding and output.

## Tonal character

For a silver-retention-inspired appearance, separate tonal contrast, reduced colorfulness, and subject readability. It is an artistic approximation rather than a measured chemical model. Keep useful shadow texture and avoid forcing the same severity onto every scene.

For a faded lower range, distinguish the black anchor from slope and separation among dark tones. A lifted anchor does not require a flat shadow segment. Inspect tonal order, fabric, and hair, and do not restore black to zero merely to fill a scope scale.

For high-key material, distinguish broad white surfaces, fabric detail, and specular highlights. If whites already work, a supported midtone adjustment may improve overall lightness without lifting every black or clipping the bright range. HDR zone names identify values rather than materials; inspect preview and overlap before adjusting them.

## Night, warm light, and neon

An existing night scene needs motivated sources and readable action within darkness, not automatic overall brightening. Preserve the difference between illuminated regions and their surroundings. For day-for-night, first judge whether shadow direction, hardness, sky detail, and subject separation can support the intended night. A blue cast and a low waveform cannot repair incompatible daylight geometry. Sky replacement or other compositing belongs outside the Color-only scope.

For warm evening light, strengthen the source relationship while preserving neutral and cooler supporting regions where appropriate. For neon, distinguish hue in the colored envelope from the transition into a bright core. Avoid giving every source and reflection the same tint or compressing them into flat clipped channels.

## Split tone and channel curves

For split tone, choose the targeted tonal ranges and how neutrals should behave. A preserved middle region does not protect an entire face. For channel-shaped color, remember that a low value in one channel is not necessarily a dark pixel. A bright red object can have low blue, so the lower part of a blue curve is not a luma mask.

Preserve monotonic channel curves unless a deliberate inversion is requested. A single middle anchor does not preserve the entire neutral ramp, and unchanged companion curves do not prove unchanged output channels when luminance compensation or downstream processing is active. Inspect skin, neutral ranges, gradients, clipping, and motion.

## Glow and highlight texture

If a glow's shape works but its bright core loses texture, bypass only that effect while preserving output to identify whether the effect caused the damage. Glow Bright Region Recovery and Halation Reduce Highlights address different additions in their respective effects; source isolation and halo saturation are different controls. If the problem remains without the effect, inspect earlier clipping and the tonal transition.

A correction before a light effect changes its source selection, while one after it also changes the completed halo. Recheck threshold and source preview after an upstream change. Recovery controls do not restore data lost in capture or an earlier processing stage. Reject white rings, flicker, unwanted tint, or muddy skin rather than judging only one attractive still.
