# Product Construction and Closure

## Read the product

Study the silhouette, seams, openings, thickness, attachment axes, and available internal space from useful angles. Identify existing internal geometry before adding anything. For an exterior-only model, form a restrained functional construction hypothesis and mark added internals as temporary artistic approximations. An exact engineering reconstruction requires verified references appropriate to that request.

Choose internal masses and connections for what the working camera can distinguish. Their positions and dimensions must fit the actual housing profile. Add detail where it makes a connection understandable. Preserve the source product's design and permanent contents.

## Make assembly legible

Show functional internal groups before closing the housing. Let neighboring groups approach while the main connection seats. Attached components should move coherently with their support, with local docking timed to the evaluated attachment position.

Repeated optical stacks can combine axial approach with turning, but rotation of a perfectly symmetric part may contribute little visible change. Judge the layered gaps and connections in the image.

Flexible circuits need an appropriate substrate and attachment direction; conductors and substrate must deform together. Continuous winding should follow the actual path of the temporary component and finish before its connection is covered.

## Keep closure clear

Choose housing pivots from their connections and test the full moving surface. A panel can fit in its final position while its rotating edge clips the frame, crosses the camera, or cuts through internal modules. Bound its rotation and approach, and time closure so the important interior action remains readable.

When closure obscures construction too early, revise its start, path, pivot, or temporary surface partition. Keep the assembly flow overlapping during the correction. Preserve the final panel shape and position.

Check internal fit against the real shell. Thin or curved covers can expose helpers that appear to be contained by their bounding box. Seat or retract temporary internals behind the covering surface before removing them.

For transparent housings, hiding helpers still matters even when they are physically inside. Return the native contents and optical appearance at the agreed endpoint. Source surface copies can hand back to native objects by functional family once their shapes and transforms match; ensure that copies and originals never remain visibly doubled.
