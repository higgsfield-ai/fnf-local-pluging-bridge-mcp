# Repeated Environment Assembly

## Local construction

Infer architectural stages from the existing structure and visible connections. Assemble supports, enclosing surfaces, openings, and finishing elements with local dependencies. Temporary framing can clarify construction where the source lacks internal structure, but native surfaces must define the completed scene.

Apply one construction technology throughout each repeated family. Distant or instanced assets still need that technology; their timing, duration, and emphasis can be reduced to suit the camera. Validate a representative mechanism before expanding it, then inspect the evaluated distant instances as well as nearby objects.

Distribute overlapping activity across the camera's visible regions. Completion of an entire category must not become a global barrier to the next category. A tiny moving detail in the distance does not establish an engaging flow after a dominant foreground event has stopped.

## Vegetation and organic structure

Derive branching from actual connectivity, thickness, depth, and attachment points. Reveal the trunk into connected branches and finer growth while retaining the source's seasonal state. Foliage appears only when it belongs to the agreed scene. Each branch should emerge from its own connection; uniform growth of the whole crown does not establish branching construction.

Inspect procedural scatter roots, prototypes, collection instances, and supporting geometry together. If the user requests vegetation removal, trace and remove its contributions only from the target scene, preserving shared resources used elsewhere. Establish the requested vegetation-free scene as the new comparison baseline.

## Enclosures and foreground surfaces

Ground and enclosing surfaces may use directional geometric reveal when their topology supports it. Moving or scaling a closed enclosure also moves its caps; an apparently safe final boundary can conceal a brief camera obstruction. Inspect the complete trajectory of walls, ceilings, foreground panels, and barriers. Exterior surfaces invisible from the working camera can remain stationary when animating them adds no useful assembly action.

## Floors, roads, and terrain

Treat a requested floor reveal as construction of the visible floor surface. Broad strips that finish before the viewer can read them may need smaller floor cassettes or planks. Derive panels from the original surface, carry its UVs and footprint, and place thickness below the finished plane. Seat the local floor before furniture feet or podium supports arrive; a whole-room floor completion barrier is unnecessary.

For moving cars, evaluate tire trajectories against the assembled road. Build subgrade and local deck supports ahead of contact while shoulders, markings, railings, and surrounding hills overlap. Ground beyond the asphalt should cover the camera's full travel corridor. Reserve an explicit assembled interval before the cut; exclude vehicle translation, wheel rotation, and camera travel from assembly retiming. Verify that all assembly channels stay settled throughout that interval.

Partition an imported landscape by its geological form: valley floor, slopes, cliff beds, and coherent spatial districts. Preserve native face coverage. Rise and settle neighboring districts with overlapping timing; avoid a stack of large floating sheets that blocks the product or camera. Use actual source topography to choose a clear valley for product docking and the camera path.
