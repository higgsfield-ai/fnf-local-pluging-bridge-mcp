# Camera and Subject Motion

## Establish the endpoint

Record the agreed framing endpoint. If the user requests a revised final composition, compare source geometry and assembled geometry through that same final camera.

Choose a continuous path that reveals useful connections at the moment they occur. Set orbit extent, distance, height, focal length, and settling time from subject scale and composition. Camera movement should support the current assembly accent and settle coherently with completion. Past shot dimensions and angles are not presets.

## Orbit the visible subject

Derive the orbit centre from the actual subject geometry. An existing tracking target may sit along the viewing ray well in front of the product. Orbiting that helper can send the product out of frame despite valid camera tracking.

Preserve the established framing offset when it is intentional. A temporary camera and temporary tracking target can move together around the actual subject centre. A semantic parent rig that returns to identity allows the original transform components and constraint relationship to be recovered without unnecessary decomposition. Verify this against the evaluated endpoint, including lens, shift, scale, and rotation.

Keep the original camera recoverable. Animate owned controllers and inspect their actual evaluated motion; assigning plausible keys is insufficient. If a copy inherits source constraints, verify their targets and transform spaces after parenting.

## Coordinate subject travel

Whole-subject travel and local assembly need separate controls. Components should approach and connect relative to the moving subject. Coordinate wheel rotation or other visible contact mechanics with translation when present, and preserve the agreed final placement. Avoid adding travel to unrelated scene elements.

## Validate the path

Project current animated geometry through the evaluated camera over the full shot, using the real aspect and pixel aspect. Check partial clipping and near-plane crossings as well as complete loss from frame. Resolve ambiguous foreground blocking with camera rays and rendered views of the affected interval.

Account for fixed backgrounds and enclosures newly exposed by the path. Repair the trajectory, target, or assembly approach within the authorized composition. Inspect meaningful stages and actual playback for readable parallax, smooth acceleration, and a deliberate finish.
